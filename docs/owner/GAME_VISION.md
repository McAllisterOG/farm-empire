# Farm Empire Game Vision

## Product promise

Farm Empire is a cozy, approachable farming-business game about building something substantial from a humble beginning. The player should start with a small patch of land and limited means, then farm, harvest, store or move crops, choose when and where to sell, reinvest, acquire better equipment and land, and gradually build a much larger agricultural operation.

The farm is a physical place the player inhabits and changes. Menus should support decisions, but the land, crops, buildings, vehicles, movement, and visible growth of the operation should remain the star.

## Desired player experience

- Farming actions feel clear, tactile, and satisfying.
- The early farm feels small, personal, and slightly inefficient.
- Storage, prices, expenses, and timing create meaningful business choices without turning the game into a spreadsheet.
- Machinery changes how work feels and how much the player can accomplish, rather than serving only as a cosmetic stat bonus.
- Land, buildings, logistics, and staff make the farm visibly busier and more capable over time.
- Progress periodically gives the player a strong sense of how far the farm has come.
- The presentation stays cozy, readable, and grounded even as the business reaches commercial scale.

## Progression fantasy

The long-form arc is:

**Start small -> farm -> harvest -> store or move crops -> sell well -> reinvest -> improve equipment -> work more efficiently -> acquire land -> operate at greater scale -> build a farm empire.**

Each major upgrade should open a new capability, remove a real constraint, or change the player's relationship to the farm. Bigger numbers alone are not enough.

## Product principles

1. **Build forward from the working game.** Farm Empire V1 is the technical and product starting point. Preserve working systems and evolve them deliberately instead of rewriting the project for theoretical cleanliness.
2. **Use older experiments only as inspiration.** Lo-Fi Farm Game, Harvest Empire, and Sunmeadow Farm are not specifications. Their code, names, values, controls, and exact mechanics have no compatibility requirement.
3. **Prioritize playable loops.** Farming, storage, markets, saving, equipment, and expansion should work together in the real game before decorative or highly complex scope is added.
4. **Make the economy matter.** Crop selection, capacity, timing, operating costs, and reinvestment should create understandable tradeoffs with visible consequences.
5. **Make scale physical.** Better machinery, larger fields, hauling, buildings, workers, and multiple properties should change what the player sees and does.
6. **Keep complexity approachable.** Depth should arrive through coherent systems and progression, not disconnected controls, admin panels, or developer-facing interfaces.
7. **Test the player-facing result.** A milestone is not complete merely because code compiles; its full workflow must be exercised in the browser and remain compatible with saved progress.

## Long-term possibility space

Potential future systems include larger fields, more crop variety, storage infrastructure, tractors and implements, trailers and combines, equipment condition and dealerships, hauling, land purchases, buildings, operating expenses, weather, workers, contracts, financing, and multiple farms or properties.

These are directions, not promises for the next milestone. They should be introduced in a sequence that strengthens the central farming-business loop and produces complete, enjoyable slices along the way.

## Experience to avoid

Farm Empire should not feel like a spreadsheet, an admin dashboard, a developer test harness, a pile of unrelated buttons, or a generic web application with farming decoration. Any necessary interface should return attention to the farm and help the player act in the world.
